# Comprehensive Guide to Mutual Fund Taxation in India

*Source: Indian Income Tax Act, 1961 (amended by Finance Act 2024), Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT)*
*Classification: Educational Information for GoalInvest Advisor*

---

## 1. Overview of Mutual Fund Taxation
Mutual funds in India are taxed based on two primary income streams:
1. **Capital Gains**: Realized profit upon redemption or sale of mutual fund units.
2. **Dividends (IDCW - Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal)**: Income distributed by mutual fund schemes to investors.

The taxation of capital gains depends strictly on:
- **Asset Classification of the Fund** (Equity-oriented, Debt-oriented, or Hybrid).
- **Holding Period** (Short-Term Capital Gains - STCG vs. Long-Term Capital Gains - LTCG).

---

## 2. Equity-Oriented Mutual Funds

### Definition
A fund that invests at least 65% of its total portfolio in equity shares of domestic Indian companies.

### Holding Period Threshold
- **Short-Term Capital Asset**: Units held for **12 months or less**.
- **Long-Term Capital Asset**: Units held for **more than 12 months**.

### Tax Rates (Post Finance Act 2024)
- **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG - Section 111A)**:
  - Taxed at a flat rate of **20%** (increased from 15% effective 23 July 2024).
  - Applicable surcharge and 4% Health and Education Cess apply.
- **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG - Section 112A)**:
  - Taxed at a flat rate of **12.5%** (increased from 10% effective 23 July 2024).
  - **Exemption Limit**: LTCG up to **₹1,25,000 per financial year** is completely exempt from income tax (increased from ₹1,00,000).
  - Gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5% without indexation benefit.

---

## 3. Debt Mutual Funds and Specified Mutual Funds

### Definition (Section 50AA)
Funds investing less than 35% in equity shares of domestic companies (e.g., Liquid Funds, Overnight Funds, Corporate Bond Funds, Banking & PSU Funds).

### Taxation Rules
- Following the Finance Act 2023 amendment (effective 1 April 2023), any investment made in Specified Mutual Funds on or after 1 April 2023 is treated as **deemed Short-Term Capital Gains regardless of holding period**.
- Capital gains are added to the investor's total gross taxable income and taxed at the **applicable marginal income tax slab rate**.
- **Indexation Benefit**: No indexation benefit is available for investments made on or after 1 April 2023.
- *Grandfathering Provision*: Units acquired prior to 31 March 2023 retain long-term indexation benefit if held for more than 36 months (taxed at 20% with indexation).

---

## 4. Hybrid and Other Mutual Funds

### Conservative Hybrid Funds
- Equity allocation between 0% and 35%: Taxed as Debt funds (at investor's slab rate).
- Equity allocation between 35% and 65%:
  - Holding period > 24 months is treated as Long-Term Capital Gains (taxed at 12.5% without indexation).
  - Holding period ≤ 24 months is treated as Short-Term Capital Gains (taxed at slab rate).

### Aggressive Hybrid & Arbitrage Funds
- Equity allocation ≥ 65%: Taxed identically to Equity Mutual Funds (12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25 Lakh, 20% STCG).

---

## 5. Taxation of Dividends (IDCW Option)
- Under the Classical Tax System (effective from FY 2020-21 onwards):
  - Dividends received from mutual funds are added to the investor's taxable income and taxed at their applicable slab rates.
  - **Tax Deducted at Source (TDS - Section 194K)**:
    - Mutual fund AMCs deduct TDS at **10%** if the total dividend paid to a resident individual in a financial year exceeds **₹5,000**.
    - If PAN is not provided, TDS is deducted at 20%.

---

## 6. Tax-Saving Mutual Funds: ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
- **Section 80C Deduction**: Investments in ELSS qualify for tax deduction up to **₹1,50,000 per financial year** under the Old Tax Regime.
- **Lock-in Period**: Mandatory 3-year lock-in period from the date of each allotment (for SIP, each installment has an independent 3-year lock-in).
- **Tax on Redemption**: Taxed as Equity Mutual Fund (LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh exemption threshold).
- Note: Under the New Tax Regime (default from FY 2023-24), Section 80C deductions are not available, but ELSS remains an attractive equity compounding avenue.

---

## 7. Systematic Investment Plans (SIP) and Capital Gains
- Each SIP installment is treated as an independent investment with its own purchase date and NAV.
- The **First-In, First-Out (FIFO)** method is applied upon redemption to determine whether an individual unit is short-term or long-term.
